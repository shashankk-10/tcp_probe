  Reading the 'over floor' column: 300 ms is an INFERENCE from two
  sysctl names, not a macro anyone has read -- TCPT_RANGESET and
  TCP_ADD_REXMTSLOP live in tcp_timer.h, which is not in reference/xnu.
  What the column actually tests is the SHAPE: if it stays flat while
  the injected delay grows, the RTO is dominated by a floor rather
  than by any measurement of this path, and the tail-loss cost in
  exp_hol is a constant of the stack, not of the network. A column
  that rises with the delay means the opposite.

  Read the '/ base' column. It should reproduce tcp_backoff[] =
  {1,2,4,8,16,32,64,64,...} (tcp_timer.c:886-887) directly.

  The raw ratio column never reaches 2.00 and even dips below
  1.0, which a monotonic backoff array cannot produce. That is an
  artefact of the constant term: because the slop is added AFTER
  the multiply -- RTO = slop + base*backoff, not
  (slop + base)*backoff -- it drags every raw ratio toward 1.
  Subtracting it first is what makes the array visible, and it is
  also why the first RTO (230 ms) sits BELOW the naive
  rtt_min+rexmt_slop guess of 300 ms: rtt_min is not the base.
